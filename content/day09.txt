DAY 9 — FINE-TUNING AND MULTIMODAL AI

1. Prompting vs RAG vs fine-tuning

Prompting:
Give instructions/context at request time. Fast to try and easy to change, but the prompt has context limits.

RAG:
Retrieve external information and add it to the prompt. Useful when the answer must refer to changing or private documents and when citations matter.

Fine-tuning:
Train a base model further on examples to change its behavior or adapt it to a task/style. It is not usually the best way to inject facts that change frequently; retrieval may be more appropriate for that.

2. Fine-tuning terms
- SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning): train on examples of desired inputs and outputs.
- PEFT (Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning): adapt only a small portion or parameterization rather than all base parameters.
- LoRA: adds trainable low-rank updates to selected model weights.
- QLoRA: combines quantization of the base model with LoRA-style fine-tuning to reduce memory requirements.
- RLHF: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback; human preferences inform a training/optimization process.
- DPO: Direct Preference Optimization; uses preference comparisons in a direct optimization approach.
These techniques differ in data needs, compute, complexity and behavior. Fine-tuning can introduce regressions, so evaluation before/after is essential.

3. Multimodal AI

Multimodal systems work with more than one modality, such as text, images, audio or video.

Image/vision:
- image classification;
- object detection;
- visual question answering;
- vision-language models (VLMs).

OCR:
Optical Character Recognition extracts text from images or scanned documents. OCR output can contain errors and should be checked when accuracy matters.

Audio:
- ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition): converts speech/audio to text.
- STT (Speech-to-Text): often used as a closely related product term.
- TTS (Text-to-Speech): generates speech from text.
- VAD (Voice Activity Detection): identifies regions likely to contain speech.
- diarization: estimates who spoke when, often assigning speaker labels.
Audio can be noisy, accents and overlapping speakers can reduce accuracy, and transcripts need validation.

Video:
May combine frame sampling/vision with audio transcription and temporal summaries. A system should preserve timestamps and not claim details that were never visible or audible.

4. AI Meeting Intelligence project
With appropriate permission for recording/transcription:
1. Accept an audio file.
2. Run speech-to-text.
3. preserve timestamps/speaker labels when supported;
4. ask an LLM to produce a summary, decisions and action items;
5. attach transcript evidence to action items;
6. let a human review the output;
7. export the result.

Important: the model may infer an action item that nobody actually agreed to. Verify action items against transcript evidence and do not record meetings without proper authorization.
